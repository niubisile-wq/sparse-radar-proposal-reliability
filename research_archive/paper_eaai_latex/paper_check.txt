Abstract
Radar-only 3D object detection is attractive for all-weather perception, but its sparse and sensor-dependent measurements make
proposal selection unusually brittle. Useful boxes can be removed by aggressive filtering, classification confidence can disagree
with localization quality, and an average improvement can conceal regressions on individual datasets or random seeds. We present a
reliability-oriented proposal refinement framework that preserves borderline candidates, recovers complementary residual proposals,
aligns confidence with three-dimensional localization quality, and refines locally consistent boxes through conservative proposal
voting. The method is evaluated on Astyx, MAN TruckScenes, V2X-Radar-V, and K-Radar using three training seeds and APR40 at
a 3D intersection-over-union threshold of 0.50. The strict voting route improves the RDAR baseline in all 12 paired dataset-seed
comparisons, with a mean gain of 0.9726 AP and a bootstrap 95% confidence interval of [0.6468, 1.3536]. A separate high-
performance route raises macro AP from 35.3552 to 39.1214, but is not assigned the no-regression claim because one TruckScenes
seed decreases. A seed-2028 progressive study, calibration diagnostics, point-dropout stress tests, threshold sensitivity, runtime
profiling, and qualitative cases connect the aggregate results to specific proposal mechanisms. The analysis shows that strict reliability
and maximum average accuracy are distinct operating objectives: proposal voting provides consistent paired gains at measurable
post-processing cost, whereas quality alignment provides larger average gains and better calibration without uniformly improving
every diagnostic condition.
