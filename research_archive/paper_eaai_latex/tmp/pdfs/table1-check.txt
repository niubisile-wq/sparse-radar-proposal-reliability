Abstract
Radar-only 3D object detection is attractive for all-weather perception, but its sparse and sensor-dependent measurements make
proposal selection unusually brittle. Useful boxes can be removed by aggressive filtering, classification confidence can disagree with
localization quality, and an average improvement can conceal regressions on individual datasets or random seeds. We formulate
proposal reliability through availability, confidence quality, and geometric consistency, and address them with a controlled refinement
framework. It preserves borderline candidates, recovers score-capped residual proposals, aligns confidence with three-dimensional
localization quality, and restricts proposal voting to supported primary boxes. The method is evaluated on Astyx, MAN TruckScenes,
V2X-Radar-V, and K-Radar using three training seeds and APR40 at a 3D intersection-over-union threshold of 0.50. The strict voting
route improves the RDAR baseline in all 12 paired dataset-seed comparisons, with a mean gain of 0.9726 AP and a bootstrap 95%
confidence interval of [0.6468, 1.3536]. A separate high-performance route raises macro AP from 35.3552 to 39.1214, but is not
assigned the no-regression claim because one TruckScenes seed decreases. A seed-2028 progressive study, calibration diagnostics,
point-dropout stress tests, threshold sensitivity, runtime profiling, and qualitative cases connect the aggregate results to specific
proposal mechanisms. The analysis shows that strict reliability and maximum average accuracy are distinct operating objectives:
proposal voting provides consistent paired gains at measurable post-processing cost, whereas quality alignment provides larger
average gains and better calibration without uniformly improving every diagnostic condition.
Keywords: Sparse radar 3D object detection, proposal preservation, proposal voting, calibration, robustness
